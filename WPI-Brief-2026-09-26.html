Top stories first.

First. France Day One is complete, and it is the densest Leo primary day of the papacy so far. After a private meeting with President Emmanuel Macron, Pope Leo the Fourteenth addressed French authorities at the Élysée. He said the protection of the right to life is the indispensable foundation of every other human right. He warned against science and technology as profit-driven ventures promoting genetic manipulation, surrogacy, the trade in human organs, or the possibility of arranging one’s own death. He defended authentic secularity that does not exclude religion. He urged France to champion a peace that is unarmed and disarming, and he repeated nuclear-ban language from Pacem in Terris. At UNESCO, forty-six years after Saint John Paul the Second, he came as a Pastor. He asked what it means to be human. He named the A.I. paradox: machines are called intelligent while persons are judged by efficiency and discarded when they are no longer productive. Peace, he said, cannot be built by treaties alone. He contrasted Babel, unity imposed through domination, with Pentecost, unity that does not erase diversity. Meaning, he said, is the oxygen of the mind. At Notre-Dame for Vespers, he called the restored cathedral a living center where heaven and earth meet. Without the liturgy, he said, a church loses its soul. He praised the Second Vatican Council’s liturgical heritage and told clergy and religious to place communion with the successor of Peter and with the Church before personal preferences. At the Stade de France, about eighty thousand young people packed a vigil. Leo answered vocation questions, told them to avoid pride, vanity, violence, and greed, to reach toward virtue, and to fight with self-giving love, not contempt. Why this matters for Where Peter Is: life, A.I., laïcité, liturgy, and youth energy landed in one day. Recommendation: a Day One map piece is now writable from the full Bollettino texts. Do not let Macron’s Mass-skip protocol eat the brief.

Next. France Day Two is live this morning. At Maison Bakhita, an Archdiocese center for migrants and refugees in Paris, Leo said no one is so poor as to have nothing to give, and no one is so rich as to have nothing to receive. He pointed to Saint Josephine Bakhita and said the poor are the very flesh of Christ. He said it is possible to live together in dignity and peace. At the Institut Catholique de Paris, with the Provincial Synodal Assembly, catechumens, and newly baptized, he called France to become a Church in a state of initiation. Protect the small flame the Spirit has kindled. Guard against post-baptismal emptiness. Let the liturgy itself form people. Accompany with closeness, compassion, and tenderness. Be missionaries of the Gospel in this society. Still ahead as of this briefing: the open-air Mass at Place de la Concorde around three p.m. Paris time, nine a.m. Eastern, with roughly six hundred thousand people registered. Then an evening flight to Lourdes, a grotto prayer tonight, and Sunday’s Mass and survivors meeting. Why this matters: Day Two is the pastoral proof of Day One’s rhetoric. Recommendation: live desk for the Concorde homily, and a possible piece pairing the initiation speech with France’s adult baptism surge.

Next. Lourdes is hours away, and the accountability frame is sharper. The sanctuary confirms that Father Marko Rupnik’s mosaics on the Basilica of the Rosary are fully covered, and will remain covered after the papal visit as a long-term measure while a permanent solution is sought. Victim advocate Antonia Sobocki said she cried with relief and will be in Lourdes. The September twenty-third open letter from five alleged Rupnik victims still has no public Holy See reply. Leo meets seven abuse survivors on Sunday. Do not conflate those invitees with the letter signatories. Why this matters: visual care, pastoral meeting, and unanswered canonical transparency converge tomorrow. Recommendation: watch through Sunday, and prep the survivors sidebar now.

Next. The Notre-Dame communion line lands against the Society of Saint Pius the Tenth schism backdrop. In July, unauthorized bishop consecrations led the Vatican to declare schism and excommunications, including for formal lay adherents. The bishop of Tarbes and Lourdes barred the Society’s October Christ the King pilgrimage from the sanctuary. France Twenty-Four packaged the schism as casting a pall over the visit. The Society publicly struck a non-confrontational tone for the trip while watching for euthanasia and abortion words, which the Élysée address already supplied. Keep this distinct from diocesan traditional Latin Mass communities and from groups in full communion. Why this matters: it is the week’s clearest liturgy and unity primary without reopening Traditionis custodes as a whole. Recommendation: a short analysis of communion with Peter in July’s context, not a culture-war scorecard.
