First. The survivors meeting that was still ahead in yesterday’s brief is complete. Sunday evening in Lourdes, from about five thirty to seven thirty, Leo met seven French survivors of clergy abuse—four men and three women—at the bishop’s residence. Bishop Gérard Le Stang of Amiens accompanied them. The Pope met each person individually. He listened to their stories, to pain sometimes compounded by the silence of those who could have helped, and to their proposals so this would not happen again. He prayed with some of them. Then he gathered the whole group. He thanked them for their painful testimony. He reaffirmed his commitment that the evil they experienced would not be repeated for others. He expressed closeness to them and, through them, to all who still suffer from the wounds of abuse. He said he hoped to work together so the community of the faithful becomes a place of comfort and healing. He asked them to pray for him and gave his blessing. Afterward, survivors spoke to a small press pool. Jérôme Guillement said there was something sincere about him, and that every word was chosen. Olivier Mardi said the Pope had done his homework, that he is a man of commitment, not naive, and that he knows vigilance must continue. Five survivors are named in Vatican News and America. Two chose anonymity. One, according to Associated Press and O.S.V., was abused by Abbé Pierre. Some French associations criticized the small individual selection instead of organization representatives. The bishops’ conference said listening requires a small room, not fifty people. Do not conflate these seven with the September twenty-third Rupnik victims’ letter signatories. That letter still has no public Holy See reply. The Rupnik mosaics remain covered. Why this matters for Where Peter Is: this is the accountability payoff of the France trip. Pair it with yesterday’s bishops vigilance language and the Mass on deep wounds. Recommendation: an accountability package piece, and watch for delayed opposition spin.

Next. Sunday afternoon at Accueil Notre-Dame, Leo addressed the sick and caregivers. Full English text is on the Vatican site. He said human dignity cannot be measured or debated. He said we must reject the temptation to cause death under the guise of false compassion. Causing death cannot be regarded as normal. What is legal is not necessarily moral. That line drew applause. Medicine’s vocation is to care for a suffering person, not to eliminate that person. Citing his June address to the Jérôme Lejeune Foundation, he said medicine must never become the servant of programmed death. Develop palliative care. Accompany the sick with Lourdes tenderness. To the sick he said: you are not a burden. You are the beating heart of our humanity and a precious treasure for the Church. Associated Press and E.W.T.N. framed this as a clear rebuke of France’s recent assisted-dying law. It also sharpens the parallel with Illinois physician-assisted suicide. Why this matters: this is the cleanest life text of Lourdes Day Three. Pair it with Friday’s Élysée, yesterday’s prairie Mass, and the bishops’ end-of-life steadfastness. Recommendation: a quick news item, and a fuller life package when bandwidth allows.

Next. Metz is live this morning. The interreligious address is already on the Vatican site in English. Leo spoke at the Robert Schuman Conference Centre. Metz is a city of borders and crossroads. Schuman turned dividing lines into bridges. Dialogue does not diminish identity. To invoke God’s name to justify violence or oppression is to profane that name. Religious discourse must call for the disarmament of hearts and words. Religions are guardians of life, especially with the vulnerable. The Europe speech on the roots of Europe for peace and unity was delivered around eleven a.m. local, about five a.m. Eastern. As of this briefing, the full English text is not yet on the speeches index. France Twenty-Four’s live blog reports key lines: war has returned even to Europe, a senseless slaughter. Negotiators must be willing to sacrifice some positions for peace. One cannot speak of Europe without mentioning Christianity. He praised integration and called Europe to integrate migrants as if they were part of one’s own family. Macron spoke of rediscovering the founders’ courage against withdrawal, hatred, and nationalism. Cathedral Mass is still ahead this afternoon. Macron will attend. Why this matters: this closes the France arc—Paris, Lourdes, then Europe and peace. Immigration lines will be the media war. Recommendation: watch for the Bollettino text, then a Metz analysis.
