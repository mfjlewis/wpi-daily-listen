First. Blessed Fulton Sheen. Yesterday afternoon at The Dome at America’s Center in Saint Louis, Cardinal Luis Antonio Tagle, as Pope Leo the Fourteenth’s legate, proclaimed the apostolic letter declaring Archbishop Fulton Sheen Blessed. He is the first U.S.-born bishop raised to the honors of the altar. About fifty thousand pilgrims were there, with nearly ninety bishops and about a thousand priests. Leo set his feast day as December ninth. The letter describes Sheen as a tireless preacher of the Gospel through the modern means of communication, and a diligent shepherd animated by missionary zeal. Tagle’s homily pivoted away from celebrity. Blessedness, he said, is communion with Jesus and with the poor, not the size of an audience. He told Sheen’s porter story to Pius the Twelfth, recalled Sheen’s Vatican Two line that only a Church wounded by poverty can convert a doubting world, and closed with this image: Blessed is the missionary who digs deep into the heart of the poor to meet Jesus there. James Fulton Engstrom, now sixteen, whose recovery was the beatification miracle, presented the jawbone relic. Monsignor Jason Gray said six purported miracles after twenty nineteen are under review for possible canonization. The full Latin letter text was not yet on the Vatican Bollettino early this morning Eastern Time. Why this matters for Where Peter Is: it is the largest U.S. evangelization and media moment of the Leo era. Tagle’s poverty and mission frame is the Vatican claim on Sheen. Recommendation: a possible article reclaiming Sheen for missionary poverty and media apostolate, not culture-war relic, plus a watch on the full letter text and the six miracles.

Next. France Day One is live. Leo’s fifth international journey opened overnight. He left Rome around eight a.m. Rome time and landed at Orly around ten a.m. Rome time, about four a.m. Eastern. President Emmanuel Macron and Brigitte Macron welcomed him. The motto is That the world may have life. Still ahead today as of this briefing: the Élysée meetings, the UNESCO address on artificial intelligence, Vespers at Notre-Dame, and a youth vigil at the Stade de France with tens of thousands of young people. On the plane Leo said he travels as a follower of Jesus Christ wanting to proclaim the peace of Christ throughout the world. Asked about White House press access limits, he said here you are all welcome. On A.I. he said the Vatican commission is in contact with leaders so that A.I. does not displace or destroy humanity, but remains a tool for human beings. Asked about Sheen, he said his family watched him on television, and that it helped them as a family. Cardinal Parolin ties the motto to life from conception to natural death and to France’s baptism surge. Andrea Tornielli wrote that the Pope is not landing in Paris to condemn anyone or barge into French politics. He comes as a Shepherd. Macron will not attend any Masses, citing laïcité. Why this matters: the highest Leo news cycle of the week just opened. Recommendation: watch. Hold major analysis for the Élysée and UNESCO texts. Do not let the Macron Mass-skip story drive the brief.

Next. The day-before bridge into UNESCO. On Thursday in the Clementine Hall, Leo addressed about one hundred forty members of the Pontifical Academy of Sciences. The theme was The Future of Science in a Changing World. Citing Magnifica humanitas, he said digitalization, A.I., and robotics bring real promise in medicine, food, energy, and knowledge. They also bring risks: surveillance, manipulation, discrimination, autonomous weapons, and heavy energy, water, and carbon costs. Scientific and technological advancement, he said, must be accompanied by a corresponding growth in responsibility. On climate, he said shocks fall hardest on the poor. The climate question is not merely environmental. It is fundamentally a question of justice, human dignity, and solidarity between peoples and generations. Move from emergency response to prevention and preparedness. Why this matters: it is the doctrinal setup for today’s UNESCO address, and continuity with Wednesday’s General Audience and Magnifica humanitas. Recommendation: a possible short explainer tying Thursday’s Academy text to UNESCO once the speech lands.

Next. The Rupnik survivors’ letter still frames Lourdes on Sunday. Five alleged victims of Father Marko Rupnik wrote Pope Leo ahead of the France trip. They welcomed Bishop Micas’s decision to cover the Lourdes mosaics as exceptionally strong and courageous. They said the Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith’s prolonged silence on the ongoing canonical trial is a further and ongoing form of violence. They learned of the proceedings from the press. A July blog falsely claimed an acquittal; the Vatican spokesman said proceedings are still ongoing. Public defamation of survivors continues. Leo is still scheduled to meet seven abuse survivors in Lourdes. Do not conflate the letter signatories with those seven invitees. Why this matters: Sunday is an accountability test, not only a Marian pilgrimage. Recommendation: watch through Lourdes, and prep a sidebar.

Next. The Syriac Mutua concordia fight. The Catholic Herald amplified Patriarch Ignatius Youssef the Third Younan’s Wednesday Pillar interview. He alleges that Cardinal Claudio Gugerotti and Bishop Flaviano Rami Al-Kabalan fostered a rebellion to oust him under Leo’s August motu proprio Mutua concordia. That law lets an Eastern synod remove a patriarch by a two-thirds vote with papal confirmation. There was still no comment overnight from Gugerotti, Al-Kabalan, the Dicastery, or the Holy See Press Office. This remains the first live test of the law. Why this matters: English-language reach of unadjudicated allegations grew while Leo is on the road. Recommendation: a possible fact-check explainer. Separate what the law says from what is alleged. Do not treat the patriarch’s charges as adjudicated fact.

Next. The White House issued a presidential message on Sheen the same day as the Mass. It celebrates him as a media pioneer who brought Christianity to a world threatened by communism, and quotes his later Up to God line against cultural Down with violence. Why this matters: it is a civil primary that will travel in U.S. Catholic politics. Contrast it with Tagle’s poverty and mission homily and with Leo’s plane memory of watching Sheen as a family. Recommendation: note the contrast in any Sheen piece. Separate the ecclesial claim from White House politicization.
