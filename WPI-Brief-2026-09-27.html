First. This morning in Lourdes, Leo addressed the French bishops. Full English text is on the Vatican site. He told them to acknowledge failings with a love of the truth and to bind wounds as the Good Shepherd. He said the Church’s voice has every right to be heard in the public square, and that it carries prophetic authority even when it is not understood. He praised their steadfastness in end-of-life debates. He said France’s hard-won balance of laïcité is gradually being called into question, so defend the freedom to evangelize, educate, worship publicly, and practice visible charity. On abuse, he thanked them for courageous measures against the painful scourge of abuse against minors by clergy or in an ecclesial context. Continue with the greatest vigilance. At the same time, support and encourage your clergy. Priests should be eagerly wanted, welcomed, and loved. On unity: no ideological divisions. Authentically Catholic sensibilities converge through obedience to legitimate pastors and especially to the Successor of Peter. Synodal style. Tradition from the Fathers through the Second Vatican Council and the post-conciliar Magisterium. Why this matters for Where Peter Is: this is the highest-value France text of the morning. Accountability, prophetic public voice, and Petrine unity in one address. It twins Friday’s Notre-Dame line about communion with Peter before personal preferences. Recommendation: a bishops-plus-Mass package piece, and watch how survivors react to the vigilance language.

Next. About one hundred fifty thousand gathered for the Lourdes Prairie Mass. Leo preached conversion and penance. Against trends that redefine the value of human life in illness, suffering, and old age, he said the grotto of Massabielle stands as a place of Christian hope. Penance, penance, penance. The Church must have the courage to begin with herself, to heal deep wounds, infidelities, and painful errors. The sick are at the heart of the Church. Leave as missionaries of hope.
