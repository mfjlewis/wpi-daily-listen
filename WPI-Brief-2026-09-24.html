First. Today is Fulton Sheen beatification day. This afternoon in Saint Louis, Cardinal Luis Antonio Tagle will preside at the Mass of Beatification as Pope Leo the Fourteenth’s legate. He will read Leo’s apostolic letter declaring Archbishop Fulton Sheen Blessed. The procession begins at one thirty p.m. Central Time. Mass is at two p.m. at The Dome at America’s Center. Organizers expect nearly forty-five thousand pilgrims from forty-eight U.S. states and twelve countries, with more than eighty bishops and hundreds of priests registered. Yesterday’s eve events included Vespers at the Cathedral Basilica of Saint Louis, with Archbishop Mitchell Rozanski and a Bishop Robert Barron homily, plus a young-adult Holy Hour at the Old Cathedral. Leo already set the frame at Wednesday’s General Audience. He held Sheen up as a holy bishop known for evangelizing through the media, and asked that his example inspire new ways of spreading the Gospel with joyful enthusiasm. Why this matters for Where Peter Is: it is the largest U.S. Church media and evangelization moment under Leo’s name this year. Controversy has been low so far. The apostolic letter text is the same-day primary to watch once the Mass begins. Recommendation: a possible quick news item after the letter drops, or a longer piece on Sheen, the media apostolate, and Tagle as legate.

Next. A major Eastern Catholic governance story. In a Wednesday Pillar interview, Syriac Catholic Patriarch Ignatius Youssef the Third Younan alleges that Cardinal Claudio Gugerotti, prefect of the Dicastery for Eastern Churches, and Bishop Flaviano Rami Al-Kabalan coordinated a rebellion to remove him under Leo’s August motu proprio Mutua concordia. That law lets an Eastern synod remove a patriarch by a two-thirds vote, with papal confirmation. Youssef says the fight began when he tried to recall Al-Kabalan from Rome to Lebanon. He claims Gugerotti pressed for the law and for his ouster, and that other Eastern patriarchs have written Leo with complaints about Gugerotti. He rejects “lack of fraternal harmony” as a grave cause. Gugerotti and Al-Kabalan did not comment to The Pillar. This is the first live test of Mutua concordia. Why this matters: it tests Leo’s relationship to Curia prefects and to Eastern autonomy. Opposition media will spin hard in both directions. Recommendation: a possible explainer or fact-check frame. Separate what the law says from what is alleged. Do not treat the patriarch’s charges as adjudicated fact.
