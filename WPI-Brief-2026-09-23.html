First. At today’s General Audience in Saint Peter’s Square, Pope Leo the Fourteenth continued his Vatican Two catechesis into chapter three of Gaudium et Spes, on man’s activity throughout the world, paragraphs thirty-three through thirty-nine. He rejected post-Christian humanisms that cast God and humanity as rivals, citing Henri de Lubac’s tragedy of atheistic humanism. He insisted that technical and scientific progress becomes authentic human progress only when it is animated by charity and ordered to human dignity, especially for the disadvantaged. He also appealed ahead of Sunday’s World Day of Migrants and Refugees for the protection of migrant minors. In the English greetings he previewed tomorrow’s Fulton Sheen beatification in Saint Louis. Why this matters for Where Peter Is: it is a same-day doctrinal frame for the France trip’s UNESCO and A.I. stop, for Magnifica humanitas, and for Migration Week. It is also an explicit Vatican Two reading against God-versus-science narratives. Recommendation: possible Where Peter Is article. A short explainer on what Leo actually said, tying the audience to France, UNESCO, and Magnifica humanitas.
