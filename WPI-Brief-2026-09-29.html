WPI Daily Roundup for Tuesday, September twenty-ninth, twenty twenty-six. Coverage since about six fifteen a.m. Eastern on Monday.

Reality check. Pope Leo the Fourteenth has finished the France journey. Paris, Lourdes, and Metz are complete. He returned to Rome Monday evening after Mass in Metz Cathedral, a private farewell with President Macron, and an in-flight press conference. What Monday’s brief still had as ahead is now on the record. Separately, a Syriac Catholic bishops’ meeting starts today that Patriarch Younan called unlawful. The full Sheen apostolic letter is still not on the Bollettino.

Top stories first.

First. The France trip closed in Metz. The Europe speech is now available in full English. Leo spoke at the Robert Schuman Centre with Macron on stage. He honored Venerable Robert Schuman. He said Europe is a cultural and historical reality before a geographic one. He named Saint Benedict as a messenger of peace and teacher of civilization. We cannot speak of Europe without mentioning Christianity. On migrants, he called for legal and safe pathways, and said welcoming someone means integrating them as if they were part of one’s own family. On war, he said war has returned even to Europe—a new senseless slaughter. Negotiators must be willing to give up some positions for the greater good of peace. That is not naïve pacifism. Guard against the illusion that rearmament is the answer. International law and keeping one’s word are the antidote to domination. To young people: love life from conception to death, start families, and engage for the common good.

That afternoon, Mass at Saint Stephen’s Cathedral. Peace is God’s gift, but a house needs human construction. Forgiveness is not weakness. It is not true that those who seek revenge are stronger than those who forgive. Reconciliation happens when someone breaks the chain of resentment. The cathedral’s stained glass, including windows by Marc Chagall, teaches that God’s light does not erase identities. The future of Europe is not uniformity, but the harmony of differences. Then farewell with Macron and the flight to Rome.

Why this matters for Where Peter Is: the France arc is primary-complete. Paris on life and artificial intelligence. Lourdes on abuse and the dignity of the sick. Metz on Europe, peace, and migration. Some wires read “positions for peace” as asking Ukraine to cede land. On the plane, Leo did not specify territory. Recommendation: assign the France wrap, and fact-check invented surrender asks.

Next. The in-flight press conference. On abuse, asked if he told Lourdes survivors the problem is systemic, Leo said he prefers a different expression. He did speak about some organizations that have, at times, as a system, been abusive. Sexual abuse is first a human problem. In the Church it wounds faith. The Church must walk with survivors toward healing. On French secularity, he said he did not cross the Church’s line. Defending life is applying the Gospel, not entering French politics. If someone calls that politics, he did that—to teach what the Church believes. On war, a few grow rich from the arms industry while billions could address hunger and housing. Keep inviting Russia and Ukraine to sit down. These wars no longer make sense. On artificial intelligence: not in panic mode; he sleeps at night. Expert warnings are not fake news. Keep working so A.I. does not remove humanity from the conversation. On Germany, he hopes to visit; only World Youth Day Korea is definite next year. Far-right politics fused with a certain religion is not authentic Christianity. The Gospel is compassion, not hatred. He called the four days almost miracles, especially Lourdes and about eighty thousand young people at the stadium.

Why this matters: the plane tape is Leo’s frame for the spin wars. Recommendation: a quick plane package and short fact-checks. Also watch Peter Thiel’s Aspen smear of the encyclical as serving Beijing. Treat that as contested rhetoric.
